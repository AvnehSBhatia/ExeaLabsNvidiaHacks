Scan a room once. Get robot training data for that room.

Walk a kitchen with an iPhone. Arrival rebuilds it in a physics engine, lets a robot arm practise there while people walk past, and hands back a dataset NVIDIA GR00T can train on.

What the robot would train on

Each run is written in the LeRobot v2.1 layout that NVIDIA GR00T reads, with an eleven-number state and action per frame plus the ego and wrist cameras. Six steps produce it.

  1. Step 1

    Scan the room

    An iPhone LiDAR scan (Apple RoomPlan) captures the walls, counters, sink, stove and fridge. The twin on this page is the sample kitchen, so you can explore it here; a real kitchen scanned on an iPhone 13 Pro Max runs through the same pipeline in the film.

  2. Step 2

    Compile the twin

    The compiler normalises the scan into fixtures with sizes, positions and colours, plus the drawers, doors and hinges they carry. Objects come from a small catalogue with mass and friction.

  3. Step 3

    Run the physics

    MuJoCo steps the room at 500 Hz. People are kinematic capsule walkers on seeded waypoint loops, and every step records their closest approach to the robot.

  4. Step 4

    Record an expert

    A scripted inverse-kinematics expert performs each task. A run counts only if the physics says it worked, such as the drawer being open or the bowl being in the sink.

  5. Step 5

    Export

    The export writes frames, states, actions and two 128 px cameras as LeRobot v2.1 in the GR00T layout.

  6. Step 6

    Validate

    A loader check reads the dataset back the way a trainer would, windows the actions, decodes the videos and reports any problems.

What it changed

Three measurements from the build, in simulation, in one room, on small samples. The film walks through each one.

39/48

GR00T learns this room

NVIDIA GR00T N1.7, fine-tuned on this room's dataset, finished 39 of 48 tasks. The same model trained on clean data alone finished 26. Paired sign test, p = 0.007.

13/15

Nemotron understands what you mean

Asked indirect things like “let me check if we're out of milk”, Nemotron 3 Super on Nebius Token Factory picked the right task 13 times out of 15. A keyword parser managed 6.

55min

A thousand demos before lunch

Arrival writes 1,000 checked demonstrations in about 55 minutes on one laptop. A person teleoperating at the measured 61 seconds each would need 17.1 hours.

All of this runs in simulation, in one room, with small samples. Real robots and more rooms come next.

Built on NVIDIA and Nebius

Each model does one job in the pipeline.

NVIDIA GR00T N1.7
The robot policy we fine-tune on the room's dataset and score.
Nemotron 3 Super
Plans each task from a plain request and the room's inventory, live on Nebius Token Factory.
Nemotron 3.5 Lightning
Writes the training and held-out wordings of every task.
NVIDIA Cosmos Transfer 2.5
Restyles simulated episodes into photoreal video, frame for frame.
Nebius Token Factory
Serves the Nemotron models the planner calls.
Tavily
Looks up each scanned appliance on the web, such as the force a fridge door needs.
MuJoCo
Runs the physics, so a demo only counts if it really worked.
Apple RoomPlan
The iPhone LiDAR scan the twin is built from.
